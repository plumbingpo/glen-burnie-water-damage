Bathroom Water Damage: What Glen Burnie Homeowners Should Know

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. We help Glen Burnie homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

A Failed Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.
